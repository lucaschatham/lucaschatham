Monster Fitness was my first serious operating role. I started in fitness and sales, then moved into account management, sales team leadership, client programming, and franchise growth.

## Problem

The franchise needed a more repeatable way to sell, retain, and serve clients. Like many local fitness businesses, too much knowledge lived in individual people: how to pitch, how to follow up, how to retain a member, how to identify an upsell, and how to turn personal training into a long-term customer relationship.

The opportunity was to turn that informal operating knowledge into a system.

## Role

I worked across sales, coaching, and operations:

- Scaled the internal sales team.
- Coached a team of 16 agents on sales technique, coaching, and contract negotiation.
- Authored and iterated a fitness franchise sales playbook.
- Built B2B relationships with local employers, public-sector groups, and community organizations.
- Developed internal upsell strategies for existing customers.
- Coached and programmed for hundreds of clients.

## Execution

The sales work started with fundamentals: clearer talk tracks, faster follow-up, better objection handling, and a more disciplined view of pipeline quality. I helped build the sales playbook so new team members were not learning only by shadowing whoever happened to be available.

The account work focused on higher-leverage relationships. I helped close B2B partnerships with organizations including Costco, Home Depot, LAPD, LAFD, AV Nurses Union, Chase Bank, Lancaster City, Kaiser Permanente, CVS Health, and Quest Diagnostics.

The retention work focused on responsiveness and fit. We implemented NPS tracking, reduced customer response time, focused on stronger customer segments, and trimmed non-essential offerings that made the service harder to deliver consistently.

## Impact

The franchise grew annual revenue by more than 3x during this period, supported by B2B partnerships, internal sales systems, and improved retention discipline. Existing customer revenue increased through upsell strategy, and NPS improved from 31 to 68.

This period also shaped the rest of my career. It taught me how much of product building is really operational clarity: define the customer, name the process, measure the weak points, train the team, and keep improving the system.

## Lessons

Monster Fitness taught me that the best process is the one a real team will actually use. A sales playbook has to be specific enough to help a new person improve, but simple enough to survive a busy day on the floor.

It also taught me that health and fitness businesses are operational businesses first. The client experience is the product.
