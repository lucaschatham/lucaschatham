At iMerit, I was Senior Product Manager for Ground Control, a unified data analytics platform for a global annotation workforce. The platform served 6,000+ annotators using 20+ labeling tools across 5 time zones, and gave internal teams and enterprise customers a clearer view of throughput, quality, anomalies, and operational risk.

## Problem

iMerit served enterprise AI customers with different data types, tools, workflows, and quality bars. The workforce was distributed. The tooling landscape was fragmented. Some data came from internal systems, some from external tools like Labelbox, Appen, and Dataloop, and some from customer-owned tools that were still maturing.

The operational question was:

How do you consolidate messy data from many labeling environments into a real-time view of workforce performance, dataset quality, and edge cases?

Without that layer, teams had to rely on spreadsheets, delayed reports, and manual escalation. That made it harder to catch quality issues early, resolve edge cases, identify best practices, and prove performance to customers.

## Role

I owned the what and the why for Ground Control, then worked closely with engineering and program management on the how and when. That included:

- Defining product vision and priorities from internal and external stakeholder feedback.
- Translating customer and operations needs into feature requirements.
- Prioritizing analytics, anomaly detection, user management, and compliance work.
- Working with engineering on implementation paths across a complex tool ecosystem.
- Keeping the platform tied to revenue, renewals, and operational efficiency rather than internal dashboard theater.

## Requirements

Ground Control needed to become a single source of truth for enterprise AI operations:

- Unified platform: consolidate data from many labeling tools and reporting surfaces.
- Scalability: support 6,000+ annotators and large-scale enterprise data operations.
- Data integration: ingest images, video, text, metadata, browser events, API feeds, manual inputs, and embedded plugin data.
- Real-time analytics: deliver near-real-time operational and customer-facing insight.
- Anomaly detection: make dataset edge cases visible, triageable, and trackable.
- Security and compliance: support ISO27001, HIPAA, GDPR, SOC2, role-based access, and data governance needs.
- Workforce management: connect performance, skill mapping, and operational planning.

## What We Built

Ground Control became an end-to-end business intelligence layer for enterprise AI data operations. The system integrated data from multiple sources, normalized operational metrics, and exposed customizable dashboards for internal teams and customers.

Key product surfaces included:

- Dynamic dashboards for throughput, quality, and project health.
- Edge Case Module workflows for capturing and resolving dataset anomalies.
- User and workforce management views for skill mapping and performance tracking.
- Automation around anomaly management and workflow configuration.
- Cloud Cover, a Chrome extension for immediate activity tracking and faster data ingestion from fragmented tool environments.

## Impact

Ground Control improved annotation workforce efficiency by giving teams earlier visibility into quality and throughput problems. That mattered for bottom-quartile management: low performers or broken workflows could be identified before they damaged contract progress.

The platform also supported enterprise revenue. Analytics and operational proof helped win, expand, and renew work with customers including John Deere and Cruise. The Edge Case Module captured and resolved 2,500+ unique dataset anomalies for Cruise, creating training and model-improvement feedback that would have been difficult to see in disconnected tools.

Across the program, Ground Control contributed more than $3 million in revenue impact and scaled data platform operations by roughly 50%.

## Lessons

The most important product lesson was that many enterprise problems look unique until you solve the first 80% correctly. A specific customer pain point often reveals a platform primitive that applies across other customers.

The second lesson was cultural: teams need shared standards for how work gets done. A data platform is not just a dashboard. It is an agreement about definitions, escalation, quality bars, and decision cadence.
