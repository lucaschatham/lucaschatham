The goal is not to recreate a commercial gym. The goal is to build a compact system that removes friction: walk in, train hard, recover, and keep the setup clean enough that it still feels inviting tomorrow.

## Why I Built It

Commercial gyms are useful, but they add hidden costs: commute time, crowded equipment, inconsistent setup, and the small psychological tax of getting out the door. A home gym gives the training environment back to the person using it.

For me, the build is about making strength training and movement practice easier to start. The best gym is the one that makes the right behavior obvious.

## Design Principles

- Keep the footprint small.
- Prioritize equipment that supports many movement patterns.
- Avoid novelty gear that solves one narrow problem.
- Make setup and teardown fast.
- Leave enough open floor space for mobility, carries, and bodyweight work.
- Store everything visibly so the room cues action instead of becoming clutter.

## Core Setup

The build centers on a few high-leverage pieces:

- A rack or wall-mounted training station.
- Adjustable weights for progressive strength work.
- Pull-up and hanging options.
- Bands, handles, and attachments for accessory work.
- A mat or open training area for mobility, floor work, and recovery.
- Conditioning tools that do not dominate the room.

The constraint is useful. When space is limited, every piece has to earn its place.

## What Makes It a Side Quest

This is part fitness project, part design problem, and part behavior system. The build has to work physically, but it also has to work operationally. If the room is annoying to reset, it will not get used. If the layout hides the most important tools, the training pattern drifts.

The best home gym is not the one with the most equipment. It is the one that makes the next session easy.

## Next

I will keep refining the setup around what I actually use: the lifts that get repeated, the accessories that fill real gaps, and the storage that keeps the space ready.

The test is simple: can this room make training feel inevitable?
